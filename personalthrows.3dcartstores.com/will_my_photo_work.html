    Will My Photo Work?

    Can you give examples?

    Ever wonder why your images print fuzzy or pixilated even when they look good on your screen? To put it simply, photos displayed on screen do not require a high resolution to appear crisp and clear where as photos printed on our products do require a high resolution to print well.

    What exactly do we mean by "resolution"? Every image is made up of lots and lots of tiny pixels, and the resolution of an image is how many of those pixels are in each inch of printing media.
    For example: we have a baby photo, that is 2514 pixels by 1755 pixels and has a resolution of 72 pixels/inch. This means that for our baby photo there will be 72 pixels within every inch...but who really measures images in pixels, we want to know what size this will print on a blanket or canvas.

    So with a little math we can see what size, in inches, this image will print.
    1755 pixels divided by 72 pixels/inch = 24.375 inches
    2514 pixels divided by 72 pixels/inch = 34.917 inches


    Lets pretend we want a big 24x30 inch canvas gallery wrap of our baby photo. The image to the left is what we viewed on screen. We don't see any issues with it because our monitors are low resolution devices so images do not require as many pixels per inch to look good on screen.

    The image to the right is a simulation of what our baby photo looks like printed on a canvas. Notice how it is less crisp looking than the image on the left? Since printers are high resolution devices your images need way more than 72 pixels per inch to look good.

    How do we make this image look good? If you want to print this photo out at 24x34inches you will probably need to take a new photo with a higher resolution camera (but that's a whole different topic) For printing materials we aren't often using photos quite this large so there is an easier way to use this photo and make it look a little better. Remember that 150 pixels per inch is the recommended resolution for printing. So lets take our same photo from above and change the pixels per inch to 150. By doing this we are also going to be reducing the size of the image in inches. In order to squeeze more pixels into every inch the photo itself will need to be smaller, how small? Lets find out.

    1755 pixels divided by 150 pixels/inch = 11.7 inches
    2514 pixels divided by 300 pixels/inch = 16.76 inches

    To get our baby photo to print crisp we can't print it out any larger than 11x16 inches.

    Image File Size Guidelines

    Your file should meet these minimum requirements, without large areas of white around the image. Small web images/thumbnails cannot be accepted.

    Minimum size needed - 4.7 Megapixels

    • File Size as JPEG/JPG - Minimum of 672 KB
    • File Size as PNG - Minimum of 5.45 MB
    • Other file types, such as PSD, AI, PDF, and EPS are acceptable so long as they meet the minimum in megapixels.

    DOs & DO NOTs Quick Guide

    DO

    • Try to upload the original file from the device it was acquired on.
    • Check your image's file size before submitting it.
    • Use a scanner to digitize hard copy photos and artwork.
    • Try to send us the highest resolution version of your image available.
    • Contact Us if you are unsure if your file(s) meets our requirements.

    DO NOT

    • Upload files that you have saved directly off of Facebook, Instagram, or other web sources.
    • Take a picture of a hard copy photo or your computer monitor with your phone or camera to acquire an image.
    • Submit a screenshot of your smartphone's photo gallery instead of the actual photo file.
    • Place your order if you're unsure if your file(s) meets our requirements.

    Tips on Taking a Great Photograph
    1. Make Background Simple.The background of your photo should be much less detailed than the subject. Thus the primary focus will be the subject. And the background needs to be a significantly different color (or else everything will just blend in too much).
    2. Choose the Scene.Make sure the background is appealing and appropriate to the subject. Since every picture tells a story, props are alright. Avoid clothing that clashes with the scene.
    3. Focus on the Subject.To determine how close to place the camera from the subject use this simple rule: the entire subject should fit nicely and FULLY into the frame.
    4. Control Sunshine and Shadows. Insufficient lighting will put too much shade on the subject's face. Improve the lighting or use a more white background to reflect the light onto the subject. The blazing sun can bleach your photo if it is not behind the camera.
    5. Use Angles to Your Advantage. A photographer's goal is to make the subject appear most natural! Rather than having a person face the camera straight-on, try to get the subject to turn his/her head and body (slightly). Avoid "mug shots" if possible.
    6. Camera and Settings. Use a 3 Megapixel camera or better at hi-resolution setting.
    What Photos Can I Use?

    Any photo you like! There are a few guidelines we'd like to offer, though.

    • High contrast- a nice, high contrast photo will always work best. If there is a good deliniation between lights and darks, good detail in the subject and a balance in contrast, your blanket or wall hanging will turn out beautifully.
    • Large Subject Matter- if your subject matter (a person, a pet, a building, etc) takes up the majority of the photo, you will be able to see more detail and the colors will be more vibrant and true-to-life. If the subject is too small, you might consider cropping the photo so that the subject takes up roughly 60-80% of the image.
    • Color- try to choose a photograph with realistic coloring. If the face in the photograph is bright pink, chances are that your PortraitWeave product will not look as realistic as you hope. Try to pick a photo with good skin tones, nice colors and one that is not too faded or old. Remember, while we will do some minor color correction and tone enhancements, what you send is what you will receive. The better and more realistic your photo is, the happier you will be with the finished product.
    • Old Photos - while we can certainly make your PortraitWeavers product from any photo, older photographs are not always the best choice. They can be faded or the color can change over time. For best results, pick the clearest, most color-rich older photograph you have.
    • Focus- please make sure that the photo you send is in focus. We can do minimal sharpening, but a crisp photo will always work best
    • Black & White Photos- we can create your PersonalThrow product from a black and white photo. No problem!
